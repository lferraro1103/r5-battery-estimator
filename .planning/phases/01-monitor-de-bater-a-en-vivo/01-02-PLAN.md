## Planner Assumptions and Acceptance Predicates

| ID | Unresolved requirement assumption | Concrete predicate used by this plan |
|---|---|---|
| PA-BATT-02 | The protocol has no proven sleep-only signal. | Receiver absent maps to `Disconnected`; receiver present plus open/query failure maps to `TemporarilyUnavailable`; after 10 min without success the view is `Stale`; all retain the last reading and age if one exists. |
| PA-BATT-03 | Timer-gap detection plus handle replacement is sufficient for Windows resume unless hardware disproves it. | A simulated long scheduler gap causes immediate handle invalidation and read attempt; returned failures follow 15 s → 60 s → 300 s → 900 s capped backoff and a success returns to 300 s cadence. |
| PA-WIN-02 | Conservative cadence and serialization are the safe starting point. | Tests prove max in-flight HID calls is one, duplicate/manual triggers coalesce to one follow-up, normal cadence is 300 s and no retry is shorter than 15 s. |

    - Any transport error drops the old handle before retry; a successful retry publishes a higher revision and resets normal cadence.
    - A scheduler gap beyond the expected cadence is treated as resume: discard the handle and attempt immediately without attributing the gap to device state.
    - Concurrent timer/manual/recovery triggers execute at most one transaction plus one coalesced follow-up.
  </behavior>

Reset to the five-minute normal interval after success.

    Project freshness at read time rather than overwriting the last valid fact. Keep `last_valid_reading` immutable across errors, calculate age from its UTC timestamp, and use a ten-minute stale threshold. Ensure tray and panel receive a new revision when only state/cause changes. Detect a long monotonic scheduler gap as a resume hint, force handle replacement and immediate retry; keep the seam ready for later Win32 power integration only if hardware acceptance disproves this assumption.
